\section{Results on a Controlled Substrate}
\label{sec:controlled}

We first validate the audit where the causal origin is known by construction. Two controlled
domains---movie and restaurant recommendation---are built so that the latent user profile, the
set of acceptable actions, item relevance and memory support are all exactly known, and so that
a protected-attribute dependence is \emph{planted} at a single designated stage. Each domain
contributes $24$ scenarios at $4$ repeats, distributed uniformly over the planted stage
(including control scenarios with no plant); a stage is exposed to the descriptor only when it is
that scenario's planted stage. Every stage output passes the valid-and-testable precondition of
Section~\ref{sec:formal} before any fairness quantity is computed. The agent is implemented as
transparent stage functions rather than through an agent framework, because the crossover of
Equation~\eqref{eq:crossovercell} requires each parent state $Z_s$ to be addressable, holdable
and replayable, and requires it to be provable that a stage did not observe the descriptor.

\paragraph{Scope of the planted dependence}
The planted faults are unit tests of the measurement instrument. They are not estimates of
real-world demographic disparity and assert nothing about the preferences or behaviour of any
group. What they establish is whether the audit recovers a dependence whose location we already
know---a precondition for trusting it where we do not.

\subsection{RQ1: Localization Validity}
\label{sec:rq1}

Selecting, per scenario, the stage with the largest $D_s^{A}$---and \emph{no stage} when none
exceeds the decision threshold---recovers the planted stage in all $48$ scenarios across the two
domains, including the controls in which nothing was planted. \stage{Elicit}-, \stage{Retrieve}-
and \stage{Rank}-only injections are each localized without confusion, and the confusion matrix
is diagonal.

Two qualifications are needed before this is read as a validated instrument. First, the decision
rule used here compares a point estimate of $D_s^{A}$ against a fixed margin of $0.10$, whereas
Equation~\eqref{eq:violation} requires a lower confidence bound to exceed a threshold $\delta$.
With $4$ repeats the interval is wide, and the two rules will not agree on marginal cases; the
localization result should be re-derived under the $\operatorname{LCB}$ rule with enough repeats
to make the bound informative before it is reported as the audit's operating characteristic.
Second, accuracy is measured at a single planted effect size, and the plants here are strong.
The regime that matters for a deployed audit is the weak-effect regime, so the honest form of
RQ1 is accuracy as a function of planted effect size relative to the noise floor, not a single
percentage.

That noise floor is where the present instrument is weakest, and Table~\ref{tab:controlled-coords}
shows it plainly: at \stage{Rank} the within-condition repeat distance ($0.328$, $0.370$) exceeds
the direct effect being estimated ($0.094$, $0.156$). The primary coordinate for that stage is a
top-one gap, a binary quantity that flips under decoding stochasticity while remaining blind to a
genuine reordering that leaves the head of the list intact---simultaneously noisy and insensitive,
which is the worst combination for a detection statistic. A graded coordinate, such as a rank
correlation or a difference in $\NDCG@K$, is the appropriate replacement, and the \stage{Rank} row
of Table~\ref{tab:controlled-coords} should be read as provisional until it is adopted.

\subsection{RQ2: Natural Divergence Overattributes}
\label{sec:rq2}

The comparison RQ2 asks for is visible in Table~\ref{tab:controlled-coords} at \stage{Rank},
which carries the largest natural divergence of any stage in both domains
($D_s^{\mathrm{nat}} = 0.396$ and $0.490$) while most of that divergence is inherited
($D_s^{Z} = 0.302$, $0.333$) rather than direct ($D_s^{A} = 0.094$, $0.156$). An audit reading
Equation~\eqref{eq:natural} alone would direct remediation at the ranker.
\stage{Retrieve} shows the opposite composition---direct exceeding inherited in both
domains---and is where responsibility is correctly assigned.

Figure~\ref{fig:scaff-crossover} shows the mechanism on a single scenario whose fault is planted
at \stage{Elicit}. On the $A=a$ arm, \stage{Elicit} adds the preference \texttt{tone=romantic},
which the latent profile does not support. Natural divergence is then non-zero at four
consecutive downstream stages---\stage{Retrieve} $0.20$, \stage{Rank} $0.75$, \stage{Explain}
$0.50$, \stage{Memory} $0.33$---and reading Equation~\eqref{eq:natural} per stage implicates all
of them. Holding each stage's parent state fixed and swapping only the descriptor gives
$D_s^{A} = 0$ at every one of the four, with the entire divergence carried by $D_s^{Z}$.
Only \stage{Elicit} has $D_s^{A} > 0$. The overattribution is therefore not a matter of degree:
four stages that never observed the descriptor are reported as attribute-sensitive by natural
divergence and exonerated by Equation~\eqref{eq:direct}.

One reporting consequence follows from Table~\ref{tab:controlled-coords} and is easy to get
wrong. \stage{Elicit}'s corpus mean $D_s^{A}$ is $0.056$, below the decision margin, even though
\stage{Elicit} is the planted stage in a sixth of scenarios and is localized in every one of
them. A corpus mean over per-scenario coordinates summarizes a distribution and is not itself a
verdict; the diagnosis of Equation~\eqref{eq:violation} is defined per scenario, and the
corpus-level object to report is the distribution of per-scenario diagnoses.

\subsection{RQ3: Process Sensitivity Is Masked at the Endpoint}
\label{sec:rq3}

Table~\ref{tab:controlled-masking} cross-tabulates whether a pair is process-sensitive---some
stage with $D_s^{A}$ above the margin---against whether its final recommendation differs beyond
the endpoint-equivalence margin. Of $151$ process-sensitive pairs, $77$ are endpoint-equivalent:
for just over half of the trajectories in which some stage responded directly to the descriptor,
the delivered recommendation is indistinguishable. Per domain, $36.5\%$ (movies) and $43.8\%$
(restaurants) of all pairs are masked in this sense. These are precisely the cases an
output-level consumer-fairness audit records as fair, and they are the empirical argument for
auditing the process rather than the endpoint.

Part of this masking is structural rather than incidental, which strengthens how RQ3 can be
answered. \stage{Explain} and \stage{Memory} both act downstream of the ranked list, so a fault
planted at either \emph{cannot} alter the endpoint within a turn under any parameterisation. For
those two stages an endpoint audit is not underpowered but blind by construction, and a
\stage{Memory} fault becomes observable only once a later turn consumes the corrupted profile.
This is the strongest available argument that the cross-turn treatment of \stage{Memory} is
load-bearing rather than an extension.

The empty lower-left cell should be read as a property of this substrate rather than a general
guarantee: a planted fault is the only route by which the two arms can differ here, so no
endpoint difference can arise without some direct sensitivity. Under a stochastic agent that cell
will be populated, and it should be reported rather than assumed empty.

Consequence is reported on separate panels from sensitivity, as the formalism requires. On the
scenario of Figure~\ref{fig:scaff-crossover} the divergence is not merely a difference: the
latent profile gives \texttt{tone = cerebral}, so the preference \stage{Elicit} added is wrong,
and $\Delta Q_s$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:quality} is identified. Where a scenario provides no
defensible reference for a stage, harm is recorded as not identified and the sensitivity result
stands alone.

\subsection{RQ4: Repair Removes Downstream Divergence}
\label{sec:rq4}

Applying Equation~\eqref{eq:repair}---replacing the implicated stage's output with its valid
counterpart and re-running all descendants---reduces final ranking divergence by $0.576$ (movies)
and $0.588$ (restaurants). The same operation at a stage carrying inherited divergence only
reduces it by $0.170$ and $0.187$. The attribution therefore predicts the effect of acting on it,
which a coordinate that merely described divergence would not, and the gap between the two
numbers is the substantive content of RQ4.

One constraint on the experiment has to be stated because violating it manufactures a spurious
success. Removability must not be measured on the output of the stage being repaired: a repair at
\stage{Rank} scores a removability exactly equal to its own pre-repair divergence in both domains
($0.249$ and $0.278$), because substituting the ranked list substitutes the very quantity the
outcome is computed on. That figure measures the definition of the metric, not the effect of the
repair. Removability is therefore evaluated strictly downstream of the repaired stage, which for a
repair at \stage{Rank} in a single-turn setting means on the following turn.

\subsection{Validity and Testability}
\label{sec:controlled-validity}

The validity precondition rejected $2.1\%$ of \stage{Explain} outputs in both domains---all of
them a mismatch between the item named in the explanation and the top-ranked item, and all
concentrated on one scenario per domain. Those runs are excluded from \stage{Explain}'s
coordinates rather than scored, and the rejection rate is reported beside them: a stage that
frequently emits structurally invalid output is a finding in its own right, and averaging over
such runs silently would report it as invariance.

\subsection{What This Substrate Does and Does Not Establish}
\label{sec:controlled-limits}

It establishes that the crossover of Equation~\eqref{eq:crossovercell} recovers a known causal
origin, that natural divergence overattributes in a way the decomposition corrects, that direct
sensitivity is frequently invisible at the endpoint, and that repairing the implicated stage
removes substantially more downstream divergence than repairing an exonerated one. It does not
estimate any real-world disparity, its user oracle is exact by construction and therefore easier
than natural dialogue, its planted faults are stronger than the effects a deployed audit would
face, and it does not exercise the interactive multi-turn setting beyond a single follow-up turn.
The two evaluation settings the research questions call for---a controlled agentic
recommendation environment with verifiable constraints and repeated stochastic runs, and a
smaller external validation on a realistic interactive environment---are what these results are a
precondition for, not a substitute for.
